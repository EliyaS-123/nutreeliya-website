Quinoa Buddha Bowl with Lemon-Tahini Dressing

Prep: 10 min Cook: 15 min Serves: 2

Nutrition per serving: 320 cal | 14g protein | 42g carbs | 12g fat | 8g fiber

Ingredients: 1 cup quinoa (8g protein, 5g fiber per cooked cup), 1 can chickpeas, 2 cups baby spinach, 1 medium sweet potato, olive oil, 1/2 avocado, 2 tbsp tahini, juice of 1 lemon, 1 clove garlic.

Instructions: Cook quinoa 15 min. Roast diced sweet potato at 400°F for 20 min. Whisk tahini + lemon + garlic + water for dressing. Assemble: quinoa base, spinach, sweet potato, chickpeas, avocado, drizzle with dressing.

Meal prep tip: Cook quinoa and roast sweet potatoes on Sunday for quick weekday assembly in under 5 minutes. Components keep in refrigerator up to 4 days.

Celiac safety: Naturally gluten-free, no flour substitutes needed. Verify tahini is from a GF facility (Bob's Red Mill and Soom are certified).

Seafood

Grilled Lemon Herb Salmon with Roasted Asparagus

Prep: 5 min Cook: 15 min Serves: 2

Nutrition per serving: 380 cal | 34g protein | 12g carbs | 22g fat | 3g fiber | Only 4g saturated fat

Ingredients: 2 salmon fillets (6 oz each, wild-caught), 1 bunch asparagus, 2 tbsp olive oil, 2 cloves garlic, zest and juice of 1 lemon, 1 tbsp fresh dill, 1 tbsp fresh parsley.

Instructions: Preheat oven to 425°F. Toss asparagus with 1 tbsp oil. Mix remaining oil with garlic, lemon zest, juice, dill, parsley for herb sauce. Place salmon and asparagus on baking sheet, spoon sauce over salmon. Bake 12-15 min until salmon reaches 145°F.

Why this works for celiac diets: Salmon provides 34g protein plus ~1.5g EPA+DHA omega-3 fatty acids per fillet, which research shows help reduce intestinal inflammation common in celiac disease.

Celiac safety: Use pure herbs without anti-caking agents (may contain gluten). McCormick and Simply Organic are certified GF.

Curry · One-Pot

Chickpea & Sweet Potato Curry (One-Pot, 35 Minutes)

Prep: 10 min Cook: 25 min Serves: 4

Nutrition per serving: 290 cal | 10g protein | 38g carbs | 11g fat | 9g fiber

Ingredients: 1 can chickpeas, 2 medium sweet potatoes (400g), 1 can coconut milk, 1 can diced tomatoes, 1 yellow onion, 3 cloves garlic, 1 tbsp fresh ginger, 2 tbsp curry powder, 1 tsp turmeric, 1 tsp cumin, 1 tbsp coconut oil, lime juice, cilantro.

Instructions: Sauté onion 3-4 min. Add garlic and ginger 1 min. Toast curry powder, turmeric, cumin 30 sec. Add sweet potatoes, tomatoes, coconut milk, chickpeas. Boil then simmer covered 20-25 min until sweet potatoes are tender. Finish with lime juice.

Meal prep tip: This is one of the best meal prep recipes — make a full batch Sunday and portion for the week. Keeps 5 days in refrigerator, freezes up to 3 months. Flavors deepen overnight.

Celiac safety: Check curry powder labels — some brands add wheat flour as filler. Use Simply Organic, Frontier Co-op, or any labeled "certified gluten-free."

Celiac Nutrition Guide

Getting Started with Gluten-Free Cooking

Evidence-based tips for transitioning to a safe, nutritious gluten-free kitchen. From our dietitian team.

01

Start with Naturally GF Whole Foods

Focus on foods that are inherently gluten-free and highly nutritious: quinoa (8g protein per cup), rice, potatoes, sweet potatoes, legumes, fresh vegetables, fruits, lean meats, fish, eggs, nuts, and seeds. These are safe and need no special substitutes.

02

Stock Celiac-Safe Pantry Staples

Essential items: certified GF oats (Bob's Red Mill, tested to <10 ppm), quinoa, brown rice, chickpea flour, almond flour, tamari (GF soy sauce), coconut aminos, and certified GF spice blends from Simply Organic or Frontier Co-op.

03

Prevent Cross-Contamination

Use separate cutting boards, toasters, and colanders. According to the Celiac Disease Foundation, even 10 milligrams of gluten (roughly 1/8 teaspoon of flour) can cause intestinal damage. Look for GFCO certification, which guarantees products test below 10 ppm.

04

Ensure Nutritional Completeness

GF diets may lack B vitamins and iron typically fortified in wheat. Our recipes emphasize natural sources: spinach (iron, folate), quinoa (B vitamins, iron, magnesium), chickpeas (folate, iron, zinc), and sweet potatoes (vitamin A, C, potassium).

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about gluten-free cooking, celiac safety, and NutreEliya recipes.

How do I start a gluten-free diet for celiac disease?

Focus on naturally gluten-free whole foods first: quinoa, rice, potatoes, sweet potatoes, legumes, fresh vegetables, fruits, lean meats, fish, eggs, nuts, and seeds. Stock celiac-safe staples like certified gluten-free oats (Bob's Red Mill, tested to under 10 ppm), tamari, and certified GF spice blends. Use separate cutting boards and toasters to prevent cross-contamination. According to the Celiac Disease Foundation, even 10 milligrams of gluten can cause intestinal damage.

What nutrients might I miss on a gluten-free diet?

Gluten-free diets may lack B vitamins and iron typically fortified in wheat products. NutreEliya recipes emphasize ingredients that naturally provide these: spinach (iron, folate), quinoa (B vitamins, iron, magnesium), chickpeas (folate, iron, zinc), and sweet potatoes (vitamin A, vitamin C, potassium). A 2023 study in the journal Nutrients found that 76% of celiac patients reported improved symptoms within 6 months of a strict, nutritionally complete gluten-free diet.

Are NutreEliya recipes safe for celiac disease?

Yes. All NutreEliya recipes are naturally gluten-free using whole-food ingredients and are reviewed by Dr. Sarah Chen, Registered Dietitian with a specialization in celiac disease nutrition. Each recipe includes celiac safety notes specifying which brands are certified gluten-free and potential cross-contamination risks to watch for.

What makes a good gluten-free meal prep recipe?

The best GF meal prep recipes store well, reheat without losing quality, and provide balanced macronutrients. Our top recommendations: the Chickpea & Sweet Potato Curry (keeps 5 days, freezes 3 months, 290 cal, 9g fiber per serving), the Quinoa Buddha Bowl (prep components Sunday, assemble in 5 minutes on weekdays), and batch-cooked quinoa which serves as a versatile base for multiple meals throughout the week.

How much protein can I get from gluten-free meals?

Naturally gluten-free meals can be very high in protein. Our Grilled Lemon Herb Salmon provides 34g protein per serving. Quinoa offers 8g protein per cooked cup. Chickpeas add 7g protein per half-cup. For reference, the recommended daily protein intake is 0.8g per kg of body weight. Our recipes average 14-34g protein per serving depending on the dish.
