Comfort-food recipes that taste like the original

- One-Skillet Chicken Enchiladas: Corn tortillas are naturally gluten-free, so this tastes like regular enchiladas. Check enchilada sauce and tortillas for certified GF labels (e.g., Siete, Mission, Las Palmas, Old El Paso) [Source 6].

Smart swaps to make any favorite dish gluten-free (so it tastes “normal”)
- Flour: Use 1:1 GF blends (King Arthur Measure for Measure, Bob’s Red Mill 1-to-1, Cup4Cup) for baking and sauces. These are designed to mimic wheat flour performance in everyday recipes [Source 6].
- Thickeners: Replace wheat flour with cornstarch, potato starch, or tapioca starch for gravies, stews, and pie fillings—these deliver the same texture and sheen [Source 6].
- Pasta: Choose GF pasta with familiar texture (Barilla GF, Jovial brown rice) and cook exactly to package directions—or 1 minute less for al dente—to avoid mushiness. Banza adds protein but has a slightly nuttier taste [Source 6].
- Soy sauce: Use tamari (San-J or Kikkoman GF) or coconut aminos in place of wheat-based soy sauce for the same savory flavor profile in stir-fries and marinades [Source 6].
- Bread/tortillas: Canyon Bakehouse (soft sandwich bread) and Siete tortillas (certified GF, dedicated facility) help replicate classic sandwiches and tacos without crumbling [Source 6].

Celiac safety notes
- Always check every ingredient is gluten-free; different GF flour mixes can yield different results, and labels can change. The National Celiac Association emphasizes verifying each ingredient and notes flour blends may perform differently across recipes [Source 4].
- Prevent cross-contamination by using separate cutting boards, toasters, and colanders. Even small amounts of gluten (around 10 mg) can cause intestinal damage in people with celiac disease. Look for certified GF seals like GFCO (<10 ppm), which is stricter than the FDA’s 20 ppm threshold [Source 6].
