Quinoa Buddha Bowl with Lemon-Tahini Dressing
Nutrition per serving: 320 cal | 14g protein | 42g carbs | 12g fat | 8g fiber
Ingredients: 1 cup quinoa (8g protein, 5g fiber per cooked cup), 1 can chickpeas, 2 cups baby spinach, 1 medium sweet potato, olive oil, 1/2 avocado, 2 tbsp tahini, juice of 1 lemon, 1 clove garlic.
Instructions: Cook quinoa 15 min. Roast diced sweet potato at 400°F for 20 min. Whisk tahini + lemon + garlic + water for dressing. Assemble: quinoa base, spinach, sweet potato, chickpeas, avocado, drizzle with dressing.
Meal prep tip: Cook quinoa and roast sweet potatoes on Sunday for quick weekday assembly in under 5 minutes. Components keep in refrigerator up to 4 days.
Celiac safety: Naturally gluten-free, no flour substitutes needed. Verify tahini is from a GF facility (Bob's Red Mill and Soom are certified).

Grilled Lemon Herb Salmon with Roasted Asparagus
Nutrition per serving: 380 cal | 34g protein | 12g carbs | 22g fat | 3g fiber | Only 4g saturated fat
Ingredients: 2 salmon fillets (6 oz each, wild-caught), 1 bunch asparagus, 2 tbsp olive oil, 2 cloves garlic, zest and juice of 1 lemon, 1 tbsp fresh dill, 1 tbsp fresh parsley.
Instructions: Preheat oven to 425°F. Toss asparagus with 1 tbsp oil. Mix remaining oil with garlic, lemon zest, juice, dill, parsley for herb sauce. Place salmon and asparagus on baking sheet, spoon sauce over salmon. Bake 12-15 min until salmon reaches 145°F.
Why this works for celiac diets: Salmon provides 34g protein plus ~1.5g EPA+DHA omega-3 fatty acids per fillet, which research shows help reduce intestinal inflammation common in celiac disease.
Celiac safety: Use pure herbs without anti-caking agents (may contain gluten). McCormick and Simply Organic are certified GF.

Chickpea & Sweet Potato Curry (One-Pot, 35 Minutes)
Nutrition per serving: 290 cal | 10g protein | 38g carbs | 11g fat | 9g fiber
Ingredients: 1 can chickpeas, 2 medium sweet potatoes (400g), 1 can coconut milk, 1 can diced tomatoes, 1 yellow onion, 3 cloves garlic, 1 tbsp fresh ginger, 2 tbsp curry powder, 1 tsp turmeric, 1 tsp cumin, 1 tbsp coconut oil, lime juice, cilantro.
Instructions: Sauté onion 3-4 min. Add garlic and ginger 1 min. Toast curry powder, turmeric, cumin 30 sec. Add sweet potatoes, tomatoes, coconut milk, chickpeas. Boil then simmer covered 20-25 min until sweet potatoes are tender. Finish with lime juice.
Meal prep tip: This is one of the best meal prep recipes — make a full batch Sunday and portion for the week. Keeps 5 days in refrigerator, freezes up to 3 months. Flavors deepen overnight.
Celiac safety: Check curry powder labels — some brands add wheat flour as filler. Use Simply Organic, Frontier Co-op, or any labeled "certified gluten-free."

Cozy Gluten-Free Comfort Food Recipes for Winter

Hearty, warming comfort food recipes — gluten-free versions of classic favorites developed so they taste just like the original. Perfect for cold weather, family dinners, and anyone who misses their favorite comfort foods after a celiac diagnosis.

Gluten-Free Mac and Cheese (Tastes Just Like the Original)
Nutrition per serving: 420 cal | 18g protein | 48g carbs | 16g fat | 2g fiber
Ingredients: 12 oz gluten-free elbow pasta (recommended: Banza chickpea pasta for extra protein, or Barilla GF for classic texture), 2 tbsp butter, 2 tbsp rice flour (or cornstarch) for the roux, 2 cups whole milk, 2 cups sharp cheddar cheese, 1/2 cup Parmesan, 1/2 tsp mustard powder, pinch of nutmeg.
Instructions: Cook GF pasta al dente (1 min less than directed — GF pasta gets mushy if overcooked). Melt butter, whisk in rice flour, cook 1 min. Gradually add milk, whisk constantly, cook 3-4 min until thickened. Remove from heat, stir in cheeses, mustard, nutmeg. Toss with pasta.
Why this tastes like regular mac and cheese: Rice flour works identically to wheat flour in a roux. Cook the GF pasta al dente and most people cannot tell the difference.
Celiac safety: Some shredded cheeses use wheat starch to prevent clumping. Tillamook and Cabot are certified GF brands.

Hearty Beef Stew (One-Pot, GF) — Perfect for Winter
Nutrition per serving: 340 cal | 28g protein | 30g carbs | 12g fat | 5g fiber
Ingredients: 1.5 lbs beef chuck (cubed), 4 medium potatoes, 3 carrots, 2 celery stalks, 1 onion, 3 cloves garlic, 4 cups beef broth (certified GF: Pacific Foods or Kitchen Basics), 1 can diced tomatoes, 2 tbsp tomato paste, 2 tbsp potato starch (GF thickener — replaces wheat flour perfectly), 2 tbsp olive oil, thyme, bay leaf.
Instructions: Brown beef in batches. Sauté onion, carrots, celery 4-5 min. Add garlic, tomato paste. Add broth, tomatoes, thyme, bay leaf, beef. Simmer covered 60-75 min. Mix potato starch with cold water, stir in to thicken. Add potatoes, cook 15 min more.
Why this is perfect winter comfort food: Rich, hearty, and warming. Potato starch creates the same thick, glossy gravy as wheat flour with zero taste difference.
Celiac safety: Many commercial beef broths contain wheat or barley. Always use certified GF broth. Pacific Foods Organic Beef Broth is GFCO certified.

One-Skillet Chicken Enchiladas (30 Minutes, Family-Friendly)
Nutrition per serving: 390 cal | 32g protein | 28g carbs | 18g fat | 4g fiber
Ingredients: 1 lb chicken breast (diced), 8 corn tortillas (cut into strips — corn tortillas are naturally GF; Mission and Guerrero are certified), 1 can enchilada sauce (Las Palmas and Old El Paso are certified GF), 1 can black beans, 1 cup Mexican cheese blend, 1/2 cup sour cream, olive oil, cumin, chili powder, cilantro, avocado.
Instructions: Cook chicken with cumin and chili powder 5-6 min. Add tortilla strips, black beans, enchilada sauce. Stir to combine. Top with cheese, cover 5 min until melted. Optional: broil 2-3 min for bubbly top. Top with sour cream, cilantro, avocado.
Why this works for families: Quick, one-pan cleanup, and kids love it. Tastes like regular enchiladas because corn tortillas are naturally gluten-free.
Celiac safety: Some corn tortillas are processed on shared wheat equipment. Siete brand is certified GF, made in a dedicated facility.

Creamy Butternut Squash Soup — Cozy Winter Favorite
Nutrition per serving: 210 cal | 4g protein | 32g carbs | 9g fat | 5g fiber
Ingredients: 1 large butternut squash (2 lbs, cubed), 1 yellow onion, 2 cloves garlic, 3 cups vegetable broth (certified GF: Pacific Foods or Swanson), 1/2 cup coconut milk, 1 tbsp olive oil, 1/2 tsp cinnamon, 1/4 tsp nutmeg, roasted pumpkin seeds for garnish.
Instructions: Sauté onion 3-4 min, add garlic 30 sec. Add squash, broth, cinnamon, nutmeg. Boil, then simmer covered 20 min until soft. Blend until silky smooth with immersion blender. Stir in coconut milk. Serve topped with pumpkin seeds.
Why this is a cozy winter favorite: Naturally creamy without any dairy or flour — butternut squash blends into a velvety, warming soup. Comfort food that's naturally gluten-free, vegan-friendly, and packed with vitamin A (over 200% daily value per serving).

How to Make Your Favorite Foods Gluten-Free (Swaps That Actually Taste Good)

Making your favorite comfort foods gluten-free is easier than you think. Here are the best substitutes recommended by our dietitian team.

GF Pasta
Barilla Gluten-Free: closest to traditional texture. Banza Chickpea Pasta: adds 25g protein per serving. Jovial Brown Rice Pasta: certified GF, organic. Key tip: always cook al dente (1 min less than directed) — GF pasta goes from perfect to mushy fast.

GF Flour Blends
King Arthur Measure for Measure: best all-purpose 1:1 substitute. Bob's Red Mill 1-to-1 Baking Flour: excellent for cookies, cakes, pancakes. Cup4Cup: professional-quality blend developed by a former Thomas Keller chef.

GF Thickeners & Soy Sauce
Cornstarch: 1 tbsp = 2 tbsp wheat flour for thickening. Potato starch: glossy, restaurant-quality gravies. Tapioca starch: best for pie fillings. For soy sauce: Tamari (San-J or Kikkoman GF) or coconut aminos.

GF Bread & Tortillas
Canyon Bakehouse: widely considered the best GF sandwich bread (soft, doesn't crumble). Siete tortillas: almond flour and cassava flour, certified GF, dedicated facility. Mission corn tortillas: naturally GF, widely available.

Getting Started with Gluten-Free Cooking

Evidence-based tips for transitioning to a safe, nutritious gluten-free kitchen. From our dietitian team.

Start with Naturally GF Whole Foods
Focus on foods that are inherently gluten-free and highly nutritious: quinoa (8g protein per cup), rice, potatoes, sweet potatoes, legumes, fresh vegetables, fruits, lean meats, fish, eggs, nuts, and seeds. These are safe and need no special substitutes.

Stock Celiac-Safe Pantry Staples
Essential items: certified GF oats (Bob's Red Mill, tested to <10 ppm), quinoa, brown rice, chickpea flour, almond flour, tamari (GF soy sauce), coconut aminos, and certified GF spice blends from Simply Organic or Frontier Co-op.

Prevent Cross-Contamination
Use separate cutting boards, toasters, and colanders. According to the Celiac Disease Foundation, even 10 milligrams of gluten (roughly 1/8 teaspoon of flour) can cause intestinal damage. Look for GFCO certification, which guarantees products test below 10 ppm.

Ensure Nutritional Completeness
GF diets may lack B vitamins and iron typically fortified in wheat. Our recipes emphasize natural sources: spinach (iron, folate), quinoa (B vitamins, iron, magnesium), chickpeas (folate, iron, zinc), and sweet potatoes (vitamin A, C, potassium).
